**Interviewer:** Luka Khachapuridze

## Warm-up

**Q1. Active projects and typical workday:** Not asked.

## Core

**Q2. Last time an inspector showed up unannounced:**
Last week. The labor inspector gave a warning, not a fine. Workers were wearing full PPE; the warning was about litter around the site.

**Q3. Last safety violation and how it was spotted:**
The litter from Q2, spotted by the labor inspector, not by anyone on the team.

**Q4. What it cost:**
That incident cost nothing (warning only). Over the last 4 months, the company paid about 150,000 GEL in fines in total, because workers kept repeating the same violations and the safety specialist was not always on site.

**Q5. What they do today when the specialist isn't there:**
They try to be strict with workers so the rules are followed. The safety specialist comes once every 4 days and tells workers what not to violate. Nothing is automated. (Not shown how they track it.)

**Q6. Belief 1: is PPE missing, or is it habit?**
PPE is almost always available on site. The issue is that workers are not comfortable wearing all of it.

**Q7. Belief 2: does the specialist leave a checklist?**
Yes, the specialist always leaves a checklist of what should be done.

## Close

**Who else should we talk to:** The company's safety specialist; he gave us the specialist's phone number directly.

**Can we contact you again:** Yes, in the next week.

---

## What we learned

- **Belief 1 is wrong:** PPE is available; the problem is workers choosing not to wear it (comfort/habit).
- **Belief 2 is wrong:** guidance exists (checklist); the gap is that nobody makes sure it is followed between visits.
- **Prediction holds so far:** compliance slips without oversight, and fines are a large, real cost.

## Ask next time

- Breakdown of the ~150,000 GEL in fines: how many fines, for which violations, over which sites?
- What happens to the checklist after the specialist leaves? Who looks at it, and when?
