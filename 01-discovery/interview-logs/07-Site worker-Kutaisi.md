### Full Interview Notes (translated into English from Georgian)

**Opening: Hi, thank you for taking the time to speak with me today. Before we begin, I'd like to ask your permission to record this session. The recording is strictly for a university project, and all data will be kept completely confidential, and no names will be used. Are you okay with me recording?**
He said he did not want to be recorded, so notes were taken instead.

**Q1. Walk me through your last full day on site, from the moment you arrived.**
We got to the site in the morning. It's just our small brigade, a few guys. Our supervisor was there too; he gives us our tasks for the day, but he works right alongside us with his own tools. We just got straight to work, did our shift, packed our stuff up, and headed home. Pretty standard day.

**Q2. When did an inspector or safety specialist last come to your site? What happened that day?**
A few weeks back, an inspector showed up. One of our mates spotted him coming in near the gate and gave us a quick sign to warn us. So we all rushed and grabbed our helmets super quick to put them on, just so we wouldn't get fined. Once he walked through and looked around, everything was fine.

**Q3. When did someone last not follow a safety rule? How was it noticed, and what happened to that person?**
To be honest, we generally don't follow those rules. We're all adults here, we know what we're doing, and we're responsible for our own lives. Even if someone isn't wearing something or skips a rule, nobody makes a scene about it. Nothing happened to him, we just carried on working.

**Q4. What did it cost: a fine, stopped work, lost days? Who dealt with it? If you don't know, who would know?**
No, it didn't cost anything. Inspections don't happen very often anyway, and we usually have cameras around where we work, so we can see when someone arrives and we're ready for them. The inspector never really checks the camera footage. I don't think they're allowed to by law, or maybe he just doesn't care enough about this job to bother. If a fine ever did happen, maybe the main office or the owner would deal with it, but we never see that side.

**Q5. Think of the last day your manager or safety specialist wasn't on site. How did you know what to do, and who checked that things were done right?**
We don't have a safety specialist, just our supervisor. When he's not around, someone else has to step up, and that's usually me. I know the project well, I know what needs to get done, so I just take charge, tell the guys the plan, and make sure the job gets finished properly.

**Q6. Do you have any safety checklist or log on site? Can you show me the last one, and who looks at it afterwards?**
No, nothing like that. Like I mentioned, we have helmets lying around, but we only put them on when we actually feel like it or if someone is watching. There's no paperwork or checklist we fill out at the end of the day.

**Q7. Who else should I talk to about this? And can I contact you again if I have more questions?**
Yeah, you can definitely reach back out to me if you need anything else. As for other people, I don't really know who else you could talk to. I mean, you can talk to my supervisor if you want. He won't have a problem with it.
