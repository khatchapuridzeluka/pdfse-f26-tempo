## Objective
Learn how daily safety oversight works on an active construction site from the point of view of a worker, and how inspector visits and violations affect the people on site.

**Our prediction:** We are wrong if site managers tell us that workers maintain full PPE compliance without oversight, or that fines from unannounced inspections are rare or negligible risks they actively ignore.

## Summary
- Workers follow instructions from a construction manager and describe PPE as basic knowledge.
- Inspector visits are sometimes unannounced. The interviewee said an inspector's manner varies, and that inspectors usually take notes and question workers.
- Consequences appear to fall on individuals: a colleague caught in a violation left the company, and the company was not fined.
- Safety on site relies on company quality control and workers reminding each other. No written tracking was mentioned.
- The interviewee considered project details, inspection frequency and fines confidential, so we collected no cost or frequency numbers.

## Surprise ★
A colleague caught in a violation left the company, but the company was not fined: the consequence landed on one person, not on the company. We had assumed that violations show up as company fines (Interview 2 reported about 150,000 GEL in four months).

## Prediction check
**Inconclusive.** The interviewee did not say that compliance is fully maintained without oversight. He also gave no information on fines or how often violations occur, so the cost side of our hypothesis remains untested.

## Insights
1. **Consequences fall on individuals.** A PPE lapse was handled as an individual error, so the worker bore the consequence and the company was not fined. Question for a manager: when does a violation become a company fine?
2. **Peer reminders are an informal control.** Workers urge each other to wear PPE. Question: who does this on days when the safety specialist is absent, and how consistent is it?
3. **Feedback is verbal.** Inspectors explain issues on the spot. Question: is anything recorded that a manager can check later?

## Limitations
- A single worker, so the findings are not representative.
- Workers are not responsible for fines or inspection schedules, so the interview could not test the cost claim.
- The interviewee ended the conversation before the closing questions, so we have no referral or follow-up permission.

## Improvements for the next interviews
1. **Match the questions to the role.** Ask managers and safety specialists about fines, frequency and costs. Ask workers about their own day, their last experience and what they see.
2. **Change route after a declined question.** For example: "What happened to the person after that?".
3. **Use open questions.** Replace "So someone not wearing a helmet is...?" with "What counts as an individual error?".
4. **Keep it conversational and reach the close.** Use short follow-ups, summarise back what we heard, and leave time for "Who else should we talk to?" and "Can we contact you again?".
