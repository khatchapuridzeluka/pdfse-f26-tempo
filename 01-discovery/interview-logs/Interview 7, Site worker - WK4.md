## Objective
Test the questions in script v2 on a worker, and learn how safety oversight works day to day on a small crew: what happens when an inspector comes, what happens when a rule is skipped, and who checks the work.

**Our prediction:** We are wrong if site managers tell us that workers maintain full PPE compliance without oversight, or that fines from unannounced inspections are rare or negligible risks they actively ignore.

## Summary
- The crew is small. A supervisor gives the day's tasks and works alongside them. There is no safety specialist.
- When an inspector came a few weeks ago, a crew member spotted him at the gate and signalled the others. Everyone quickly put their helmets on "so we wouldn't get fined". Nothing was found.
- The interviewee said the crew generally does not follow the safety rules. When someone skips one, nothing happens to him.
- Nothing has ever cost the crew anything. He believes inspections are rare, and the crew sees inspectors coming (a lookout, and cameras around the work area).
- There is no checklist, log or paperwork. When the supervisor is away, an experienced worker takes charge of the task plan.
- He did not know who handles fines. He guessed the main office or the owner.

## Surprise ★
Compliance is staged for the inspector, not kept up. The crew has an early warning (a lookout at the gate, cameras), puts helmets on only when someone is watching, and sees skipping a rule as a personal choice with no consequence. We expected workers to describe a risk they take seriously; he described one they have learned to avoid.

## Prediction check
**The prediction is not contradicted.** He raised helmets and fines himself while answering the inspector question, before we asked about PPE. He says compliance depends on being watched, and that nothing has cost the crew anything. We have only one worker's account, and he is not responsible for fines.

## Insights
1. **Compliance appears when the inspector appears.** A warning at the gate was enough to switch it on. Question for a manager: how do you know what happens between inspections?
2. **Skipping a rule has no consequence inside the crew.** Nothing happened to the person. Compare Interview 6, where a colleague caught in a violation left the company. Question: what decides whether a violation has a consequence?
3. **Fines are invisible to the crew.** They are handled by the main office or the owner, which fits what the owner told us in Interview 2. Question for an owner: does the crew ever find out why a fine happened?
4. **There is no written record.** No checklist or log exists, and oversight is the supervisor alone.
5. **Script v2 worked on a worker.** Past-event questions gave concrete answers where Interview 6 hit "confidential". The "show me the last checklist" question got a clear answer.

## Limitations
- A single worker in a small crew, so the findings are not representative.
- No recording. The quotes are reconstructed from notes, so they are not verbatim.
- The statements about cameras and inspectors' powers are his beliefs and are unchecked.
- We do not know how often inspectors actually come, or what a fine costs this crew.
- The site and company are not recorded in this log.

## Improvements for the next interviews
1. **Record the exact words of key answers** when recording is declined, with the note-taker marking them as verbatim.
2. **Ask for dates and numbers.** For example, "How many times did an inspector come this year?" and "What did the last fine cost the company?"
3. **Ask the supervisor the same past-event questions** and compare the two accounts.
