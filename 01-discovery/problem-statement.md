## Executive Summary

**One-Sentence Problem Statement:**

Owners of mid-sized construction companies in Georgia (Log 2) pay large fines for simple, repeated safety violations (Log 2) because on the days between their safety specialist's visits nobody checks the site (Logs 2, 3), so edge fences removed for lifting stay off (Logs 3, 5), uncomfortable PPE goes unworn (Log 2), and litter builds up (Logs 2, 5) until a labor inspector finds it (Log 2).

**Confidence Level:** ⭐⭐⭐ (3/5)

**Why this confidence level:**
The problem is described consistently from three sides: the owner who pays (Log 2), the specialist who checks (Log 3) and the team lead whose crew causes the violation (Log 5). But it rests on 3 interviews, two of them from the same company (Logs 2, 3), and only one gives a cost figure (Log 2).

## Problem Statement Components

### Component 1: Specific Segment (WHO)

**WHO exactly experiences this problem?**

Owners of mid-sized construction companies in Georgia running active building sites, who hire a labor safety specialist that visits each site only every few days and who pay the fines when inspectors find violations (Logs 2, 3).

**ICP Validation from Interviews:**
- 1/3 interviews matched our ICP directly: the owner who pays (Log 2).
- 2/3 were close matches: people on the same sites rather than the owner, the specialist (Log 3) and a team lead (Log 5).
- Refinement: the ICP is the owner, but the problem happens through the specialist and the site team, so they should be interviewed too.

**Refinements to ICP Based on Discovery:**
- **Original ICP:** Construction company owners and site managers in Georgia whose workers skip PPE between specialist visits.
- **Validated ICP:** Owners of mid-sized companies with a visiting safety specialist (Logs 2, 3); the violations are not only PPE but edge fences and litter (Logs 2, 3, 5).
- **Key learnings:** The owner pays, but does not see the violations; they happen on the team lead's side during normal work (Log 5).

**Evidence:**
- Interview #2: Matches ICP. Owner of a mid-sized construction company in Tbilisi; ~150,000 GEL in fines over 4 months.
- Interview #3: Close match. The same company's safety specialist; visits sites periodically and cannot be there all the time.
- Interview #5: Close match. Construction team lead in Tbilisi; his crew removed a fence for lifting and didn't put it back.

### Component 2: Specific Problem (WHAT)

**From Pattern #1 (top-ranked pattern from analysis):**

**WHAT problem do they experience?**

Safety slips on the days between the specialist's visits, because nobody checks the site: the specialist comes once every 4 days, leaves a checklist, and nobody follows it up until he comes back (Logs 2, 3).

**Pattern Validation:**
- Mentioned in: 2/3 interviews (Logs 2, 3)
- Supporting insights: 4
- Intensity rating: 4/5 (largest cost: ~150,000 GEL in 4 months)

**Key Evidence Quotes (paraphrased):**
1. Interview #2: "In the last four months we've paid about 150,000 GEL in fines, because the workers keep repeating the same violations and the safety specialist isn't always on site."
2. Interview #2: "Yes, the specialist always leaves a checklist of what should be done."
3. Interview #3: "I can't be on site 24 hours a day or watch for simple violations like this every moment."

**Supporting Patterns:**
- Pattern #2: Protection is available on site but gets undone during normal work. Mentioned in 3/3 interviews (Logs 2, 3, 5).
- Pattern #3: Site-order violations like litter count too, not only PPE and fences. Mentioned in 2/3 interviews (Logs 2, 5).

**How patterns relate:**
Pattern #2 is how violations start: fences come off for lifting and PPE comes off for comfort (Logs 2, 3, 5). Pattern #1 is why they stay: nobody checks between visits (Logs 2, 3). Pattern #3 shows the inspector then finds them across the whole site, litter included (Logs 2, 5).

### Component 3: Context (WHEN/WHERE)

**WHEN/WHERE does this problem occur?**

On active construction sites, on the days between the safety specialist's visits (Logs 2, 3), during normal work such as lifting materials, when a fence is removed and nobody puts it back (Logs 3, 5).

**Contextual Evidence:**
- Interview #2 described: the specialist comes once every 4 days; the last violation (litter) was found by the labor inspector, not by the team.
- Interview #3 described: edge fences are removed while lifting materials and not put back; he can't be on site all the time.
- Interview #5 described: his team took a fence off to lift something up, and nobody put it back.

**Frequency:**
- Every few days (between each specialist visit): Logs 2, 3
- Described as the "most common" violation: Log 3
- Exact daily/weekly frequency: not measured yet

**Typical Scenarios Where Problem Occurs:**
1. Lifting materials at a slab edge: the fence is removed and left off (Logs 3, 5).
2. Workers skipping PPE they find uncomfortable (Log 2).
3. Litter left around the site (Logs 2, 5).

### Component 4: Root Cause (WHY)

**WHY does this problem exist at the deepest level?**

Because safety on site is checked only when the specialist visits, and between visits nobody is responsible for checking that protection is back in place and the checklist is followed (Logs 2, 3).

**Five Whys Chain:**
```
Surface: Companies get fined and warned by labor inspectors (Log 2)
  ↓ Why?
Violations are on site when the inspector arrives: fences missing, PPE not worn, litter (Logs 2, 3, 5)
  ↓ Why?
Protection is removed during normal work and not put back (Logs 3, 5); PPE is uncomfortable (Log 2)
  ↓ Why?
Nobody notices it is missing afterwards (Logs 2, 3, 5)
  ↓ Why?
The specialist only visits every 4 days and cannot watch all the time (Logs 2, 3)
  ↓ Why?
ROOT CAUSE: Between visits, nobody is responsible for following up the specialist's checklist (Log 2)
```

**Evidence of Root Cause (paraphrased):**
- Interview #2: the specialist leaves a checklist, but nobody makes sure it is followed between visits.
- Interview #3: "I can't be on site 24 hours a day or watch for simple violations like this every moment."
- Interview #5: "We took the fence off to lift something up, and then nobody put it back."

**How we validated this is the root cause:**
It explains all three patterns, and it rules out the other causes we believed: PPE is available (Log 2) and guidance exists (Log 2), so the gap is follow-up, not equipment or instructions.

**Surface problems this root cause explains:**
1. Edge fences left off after lifting (Logs 3, 5)
2. PPE not worn (Log 2)
3. Litter around the site (Logs 2, 5)

## FINAL PROBLEM STATEMENT

### Complete Statement

Owners of mid-sized construction companies in Georgia that rely on a visiting safety specialist (Logs 2, 3) face repeated, simple safety violations (edge fences removed for lifting and not put back, PPE not worn, litter on site) (Logs 2, 3, 5) on the days between the specialist's visits (Logs 2, 3), because between visits nobody is responsible for following up the specialist's checklist and checking that protection is back in place (Logs 2, 3), resulting in about 150,000 GEL in fines over 4 months (Log 2) and inspection warnings even when PPE is worn (Log 2).

**Evidence documented across 3 customer discovery interviews** conducted in Weeks 3-4 (4–9 October 2026), from Interviews #2, #3, #5. Top pain points identified: (1) safety slips between the specialist's visits, (2) protection gets undone during normal work, (3) site-order violations count too. Root cause analysis completed using Five Whys.

### What Changed & Why

1. **Specificity Increased:**
   - Before: "PPE compliance"
   - After: Edge fences removed for lifting, PPE not worn, litter
   - Why: The specialist and team lead named edge fences as the main violation (Logs 3, 5)

2. **Quantification Added:**
   - Before: "Thousands of GEL"
   - After: About 150,000 GEL over 4 months
   - Why: The owner gave the figure (Log 2)

3. **Root Cause Identified:**
   - Before: Workers skip PPE when the specialist is away
   - After: Nobody is responsible for follow-up between visits
   - Why: PPE and guidance already exist (Log 2); the gap is checking (Logs 2, 3)

4. **ICP Refined:**
   - Before: Owners and site managers
   - After: Owners of mid-sized companies with a visiting specialist, with the problem happening on the team lead's side
   - Why: Who pays (Log 2) differs from where violations happen (Log 5)

**What Surprised Us:**
The most common violation is edge fences, not PPE (Logs 3, 5). And violations come from forgetting during normal work, not from refusing the rules (Log 5).

**What Got Invalidated:**
That PPE is missing or too expensive (Log 2), and that the specialist gives no guidance (Log 2).

**What Got Strongly Validated:**
That compliance slips without oversight, and that fines are a large, real cost (Logs 2, 3).

## Next Phase: Design Direction

**Based on this validated problem, our solution MUST:**

1. **Work on the days the specialist is not on site**
   - Why: That is when violations happen and stay (root cause)
   - Evidence: Logs 2, 3

2. **Catch protection that was removed during work, e.g. a fence after lifting**
   - Why: The most common violation is undone safety, not missing safety
   - Evidence: Logs 3, 5

3. **Turn the specialist's checklist into something that gets followed up between visits**
   - Why: Guidance exists but nobody checks it
   - Evidence: Log 2

**Based on this validated problem, our solution SHOULD:**
- Cover site order (litter), not only PPE and fences (Logs 2, 5)
- Show the owner what is happening on site, since he pays but isn't there (Log 2)

**Based on this validated problem, our solution MUST NOT:**
- Assume equipment or guidance is missing: both exist (Log 2)
- Depend on the specialist being on site all the time (Log 3)

**Initial Solution Direction (High-Level):**
Something that keeps checking the site between the specialist's visits and flags when protection has been removed and not restored, so it is fixed before an inspector finds it.
