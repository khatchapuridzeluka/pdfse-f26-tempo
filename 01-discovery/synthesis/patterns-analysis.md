# Patterns Analysis

**Date:** 18 October 2026
**Interviews analyzed:** 2, 3, 5
**Source:** [affinity-map.md](affinity-map.md)

Each pattern is a sentence about what the evidence says, cited by interview number. Only statements made by the interviewees are used; answers marked *(assumption)* or *(from Interview 2)* in the logs are not counted.

---

## Pattern 1: Safety slips in the days between the specialist's visits, because nobody is checking

**Interviews:** 2, 3 (2 of 3)

**Evidence:**
- Interview 2: the specialist comes once every 4 days; nothing is checked in between, and his checklist is not followed up.
- Interview 2: about 150,000 GEL in fines over 4 months, from repeated violations while the specialist wasn't on site.
- Interview 3: the specialist says he cannot be on site 24 hours a day or watch for simple violations every moment.

**Intensity:** High. It is the most expensive pattern (150,000 GEL in 4 months), and the owner and the specialist both describe it.

**Root cause:** Oversight is periodic, but violations happen continuously. Nobody between the owner and the workers is responsible for checking safety every day.

---

## Pattern 2: Protection is available on site but gets undone during normal work

**Interviews:** 2, 3, 5 (3 of 3)

**Evidence:**
- Interview 2: PPE is almost always on site; workers don't wear all of it because it is uncomfortable.
- Interview 3: missing edge fences are the most common violation; they are removed while lifting materials and not put back.
- Interview 5: the team removed a fence to lift materials and nobody put it back.

**Intensity:** High. The safety specialist calls it the most common violation, and the team lead describes it happening on his own team.

**Root cause:** Safety equipment gets in the way of real work (lifting, moving, comfort), so it comes off. Restoring it is nobody's specific job, so it stays off.

---

## Pattern 3: Site-order violations like litter count too, not only PPE and fences

**Interviews:** 2, 5 (2 of 3)

**Evidence:**
- Interview 2: the latest inspection gave a warning for litter around the site, while workers were wearing full PPE.
- Interview 5: the team lead names litter around the site as a common violation.

**Intensity:** Medium. The litter incident was a warning, not a fine, but it shows inspectors check the whole site.

**Root cause:** Safety is usually thought of as PPE, so site order gets less attention, even though inspectors check it.

## How the patterns connect

Protection gets removed during work (Pattern 2) → nobody checks between visits (Pattern 1) → an inspector finds it, including site-order issues (Pattern 3) → warnings and fines.
